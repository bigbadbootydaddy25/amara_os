## County-Level Overview

### Harris County (Houston Metro)

**Historical Rank:** #1 in Texas by absolute monthly trustee-sale volume

Harris County is the **highest-volume foreclosure county in Texas** by a substantial margin, driven by:
- Population size (4.7+ million residents — largest county in Texas)
- High rate of homeownership relative to median income
- Economic cycles tied to energy sector employment
- Large inventory of homes built in the 1970s–1990s at lower price points
- Historically higher concentrations of subprime and FHA mortgages in specific ZIP codes

**Typical Monthly Trustee-Sale Volume:** 1,000–2,500+ notices filed (varies significantly with economic conditions)

**Market Characteristics:**
- Wide geographic spread — Houston is one of the most sprawling cities in the US
- Inner Loop (610 Loop) properties often have higher equity; foreclosure concentration is higher in the outer rings and specific northeast/southeast corridors
- The northeast quadrant (near Loop 610 East, I-10 East, US-59 South corridors) shows historically elevated distressed property concentrations

### Dallas County (Dallas Metro)

**Historical Rank:** #2 in Texas by monthly trustee-sale volume

Dallas County is the second-highest foreclosure county in Texas, driven by:
- Large population (2.6+ million) with significant affordable housing stock
- Southern and southeastern Dallas has long experienced higher mortgage default rates
- The I-45 and I-20 corridors in southern Dallas are historically distressed
- Dallas has a large renter population, and investor-owned properties entering distress are a consistent segment

**Typical Monthly Trustee-Sale Volume:** 500–1,200+ notices filed

### Tarrant County (Fort Worth / Arlington Metro)

- **Primary targets:** High-activity ZIPs for maximum lead volume

### For Hedge-Fund Portfolio Strategy

ZIP-level concentration analysis is a key input for portfolio geographic diversification. High-activity ZIPs provide:
- Higher deal velocity (more opportunities per month)
- More reliable comparables for AVM validation
- Higher liquidity at resale (more buyer activity)

However, excessive concentration in a single ZIP or neighborhood creates correlation risk — properties in the same micro-market tend to be affected by the same economic and neighborhood trends simultaneously.
